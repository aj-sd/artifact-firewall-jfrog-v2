Why Artifact Firewall

Block the known. Quarantine the unknown.

Scanners like JFrog Xray only catch vulnerabilities after dependencies are pulled in, by which point it is too late. Artifact Firewall enforces policy the moment a package is requested, stopping malware and vulnerable versions before they are installed, across npm, PyPI, Maven, and NuGet.

  • Block
    Known risks
    Deny versions matched by OSV intelligence or your own rules, returning a clear policy reason.
  • Hide
    Risky versions
    Demote risky versions, such as medium-severity ones, so they no longer count as latest. The latest becomes the latest approved version, while a pinned version still installs.
  • Quarantine
    Unknown packages
    Hold brand-new versions for a configurable window, long enough for the community to flag malicious uploads.
  • Serve
    Approved packages
    Pass approved versions straight through, so builds resolve to a safe release.

Frictionless for developers

Risky versions are hidden and safe, approved ones served in their place, so existing workflows keep running without failing builds.

Compliance & governance

Namespace isolation, time-based quarantine windows, and policy-as-code give you the controls the EU Cyber Resilience Act and corporate auditors expect.

One policy layer in front of the registries you already run

Artifact Firewall enforces in the request path, so your existing registries, repository managers, and package clients keep working unchanged. It adds a policy layer in front of your stack, rather than replacing any of it.

Coming from JFrog

Keep your registries. Drop the lock-in.

Scanners flag risks after the download. To block at request time, JFrog now points you at Curation, a capability bound to its own platform. Artifact Firewall gives you that same request-time enforcement without adopting a platform to get it.

The platform-locked path

JFrog Curation

  • !Request-time blocking is moving out of Xray and into Curation through 2026.
  • !Runs only on the JFrog platform: Artifactory, Xray, and the Catalog service.
  • !Registry, scanner, and policy gate all come from, and bill through, one vendor.
The vendor-neutral path

Varnish Artifact Firewall

  • ✓Request-time enforcement as a standalone layer, no platform to adopt.
  • ✓Sits in front of Artifactory, Nexus, or whatever registries you already run.
  • ✓Keep your stack. Add enforcement in the path, with no rip-and-replace.
